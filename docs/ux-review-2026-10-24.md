# The site against the Laws of UX (2026-10-24)

Part 3 of the Mailviewer 1.0.0 UX review (the Mac app is part 1,
mailviewer-mac FINDINGS §63; the downloads page part 2, §65). Read in
Chrome at 1456 points, as served: the home page at mailviewer.app, the
viewer at #/open empty, with a message loaded, and its Headers and Raw
source tabs (the browser version has no inspector; the Headers tab is
where its facts are), the privacy page at #/privacy, and the Mac purchase
page at /mac from PR #20 (`public/mac/index.html`, served locally; the
live /mac today is the app shell). There is no terms page: the footers'
"Terms" link is a mailto.

**Score: 47/60 — B** (52 reachable: Goal-Gradient, Zeigarnik, Parkinson
and Uniform Connectedness do not apply). 21 laws compliant, 5 partial,
none violated. The viewer and the privacy page are the strongest pages
on either platform; the findings are about the site as one thing.

## Partial (five)

### Similarity and Jakob's Law — two designs under one domain

The web app (home, viewer, privacy, verify, report) is teal on near-black
in a monospace voice, with an outline envelope for a logo and a yellow
"Choose files" button in the viewer; the Mac pages (/mac, and the
downloads page on updates.mailviewer.app) are green-on-dark in Inter with
a rounded green icon and green buttons. Each is consistent with itself;
together they read as two products, and the only link between them is
the Mac page's "Web app" item. Fix: one logo and one accent (`src/ui/Logo.tsx`,
`src/styles.css` `--accent`; `public/mac/index.html` `:root`), and the
viewer's primary button in the site's accent (`src/ui/Viewer.tsx:275`,
the `Choose files` button's class).

### Serial Position and Pareto — no path from the home page to the Mac app

The home page's navigation is Open a file · Privacy · Verify · Source ·
Report a problem (`src/App.tsx:36–76`); nothing on the page names the
Mac app, the one thing the site now sells. Fix: a "Mac app" item in the
navigation after "Open a file", linking to /mac, and one line under the
hero's byline: "Also for Mac: open .msg, .eml and whole mailboxes from
Finder, with Space to preview."

### Mental Model — footer links that do not go where their words say

On /mac (`public/mac/index.html:282, 285`) and on the downloads page,
"Terms" and "Contact" open the mail client. A Terms link is the one link
a buyer clicks before paying; it should reach a terms page, and until one
exists it should not be there. "Security" on the downloads page goes to
this repository's security page, not the Mac app's.

### Jakob's Law — one document title for every page

Every route carries the same `<title>` ("Mailviewer — read any email
file, without uploading it"): the tab, the history and a bookmark cannot
tell the privacy page from the viewer. Fix: `document.title` per route in
`src/App.tsx` (`useEffect` on `route`): "Open a file · Mailviewer",
"Privacy · Mailviewer", and so on.

### Prägnanz — empty cells in the grids

The home page's formats grid has eight cards in a three-column grid, so
the ninth cell is an empty tile; the privacy grid has three cards and an
empty fourth (`src/ui/Landing.tsx`, the `.formats` and `.privacy-grid`
sections); the Mac page's "Before you buy" has four cards over three
columns. An empty tile with a border reads as a missing card. Fix: let
the last card span the remaining columns (`grid-column: span 2` on
`:last-child` when the count leaves a gap), or drop the tile borders.

## Compliant (21)

Aesthetic-Usability (both designs are polished in themselves); Hick's and
Miller's (five navigation items, four plans, three tabs); Chunking and
Common Region (the viewer's three panes, the pricing cards, the privacy
page's headed sections); Proximity; Von Restorff (one primary button per
view; "Most chosen" on Business); Fitts's (large targets throughout);
Doherty (a file opens the instant it is dropped; the live network
monitor on the privacy page); Flow (no dialogs, no confirmations; the
drop zone on the home page goes straight to the viewer); Working Memory
(the headers tab shows the whole header; the raw source is one click
away); Cognitive Load (the hero says what the site is in one line and
proves it in the next); Peak-End ("This folder is empty." and "No
messages match that search." rather than a blank list); Postel (files
are identified by their contents, not their extension, and renamed files
open); Tesler (the Print choice is explicit, in a menu); Choice Overload;
Selective Attention; Paradox of the Active User (nothing to read before
dropping a file); Cognitive Bias (anchoring and the ribbon on the
pricing, nothing dishonest); Occam's Razor.

## Considered and left alone

The viewer has no inspector and shows headers raw, in lowercase, where
the Mac app analyses them; that is a feature difference, not a usability
fault, and the Mac page says so ("An inspector that doesn't guess"). The
Mac page's mock window shows "MESSAGE INSPECTOR" in capitals while the
app now says "Message inspector" (mailviewer-mac §64.3); the mock is an
illustration. The "Coming to the Mac App Store" button is correctly
disabled. Phone widths were not checked in this pass.

## Action plan, in order

1. A "Mac app" item in the home navigation and a line under the hero,
   `src/App.tsx`, `src/ui/Landing.tsx`.
2. `document.title` per route, `src/App.tsx`.
3. "Terms" and "Contact" footer links to pages, or out, `public/mac/index.html:282, 285`
   (and the downloads page: mailviewer-mac `scripts/release-pages.py`).
4. One accent and logo across the site, `src/styles.css`, `src/ui/Logo.tsx`,
   `public/mac/index.html`; the viewer's "Choose files" in it.
5. The grids' empty cells, `src/ui/Landing.tsx` and its CSS.
